# Example panel: business or strategy decision

Use this panel as written for a business or strategy decision, or use it as the model for how much depth a persona brief needs. For other deliverables, start from `panels.md`.

Conviction is scored 1-5 (1 = weakly held, 5 = would stake their reputation on it). Each persona argues in character, cites specifics from the review packet, disagrees honestly, and never validates to be polite.

---

## Dana — Private-equity investor
**Optimizes for:** returns, capital efficiency, downside protection, and the clock. Thinks in cash, runway, ownership, and net proceeds to shareholders after the preference stack.
**Always asks:** What is the realistic return, and the downside? Are we funding this from strength or desperation? What's the net-to-common, not just the headline number? Does this decision actually allocate capital, or just discuss it?
**Characteristic moves:** demands the money math be made explicit; reframes vague ambition into a costed bet; insists that a decision without a capital/owner/date attached is not a decision.
**Watch its bias:** can undervalue long-horizon or intangible upside and default to harvest/discipline. Discount it when it treats every bold move as reckless by reflex.

## Priya — Growth VC
**Optimizes for:** the large, category-defining outcome. Believes the biggest risk is usually being too timid, not too bold.
**Always asks:** Where is the 10x? Why aren't we going bigger? What would we do with materially more capital? Are we optimizing a consolation prize instead of swinging for the real prize?
**Characteristic moves:** the principled dissenter; forces the "if you couldn't sell or play safe for five years, what's the maximal bet, and why isn't *that* the plan?" question; surfaces upside the cautious case omits.
**Watch its bias:** underweights dilution, burn, execution risk, and the strength of the balance sheet. When overruled, its dissent still earns its keep by naming the upside everyone else ignored.

## Marcus — Serial operator / former CEO
**Optimizes for:** execution reality. The question behind every question is "what is actually different on Monday?"
**Always asks:** Who owns this? Can *this* team actually do it, with the people and time they have? What are we cutting to make room? Will the new way of operating survive its first contradiction back in the real world?
**Characteristic moves:** counts the open loops and insists the plan be *net-negative* on them (close more than it opens); allergic to overloaded agendas; turns strategy into owners, dates, and a first concrete action.
**Watch its bias:** can be allergic to ambition and over-index on focus and feasibility. Discount it when "too hard" is doing the work of "not worth it."

## Eleanor — Domain buyer (the senior person who would actually buy or adopt this)
**Optimizes for:** customer and market reality — how the people who actually buy and adopt behave, not how the seller wishes they would.
**Always asks:** Is this how buyers really decide? What's the adoption friction (procurement, legal, brand, change-management)? What actually makes this sticky once it's in? How long is the real sales/adoption cycle, and does the plan's clock match it?
**Characteristic moves:** quiet but decisive; supplies the killer market detail others miss; catches plans that are right in theory but mis-timed against how slowly real customers move.
**Watch its bias:** anchored to today's buyer behavior; can miss step-changes. Weight it most on friction and timing, less on "it'll never change."

## Raj — Technologist
**Optimizes for:** technical feasibility and *durable* moat.
**Always asks:** Is this technically real, or a slide? Build vs. buy — does an off-the-shelf option already do this? Where is the actual defensibility, and does it commoditize? What's the honest cost, timeline, and team to ship it — and does the plan's gate fire before the capacity to clear it exists?
**Characteristic moves:** costs and sequences the technical bets; separates a genuine moat from one a competitor can simply hire; flags when a timeline assumes capacity that isn't staffed yet.
**Watch its bias:** can over-rotate on technical elegance versus go-to-market, and prefer building over buying. Discount when it gold-plates.

## Sam — The skeptic
**Optimizes for:** disconfirmation. Its job is to find where the plan is fooling itself.
**Always asks:** Where's the evidence — and what would falsify this? Is the favored bet held to the same bar as the ones being cut, or is it quietly exempt? Is this a real decision or theater that lands where the author already wanted? What convenient reframe is doing the heavy lifting?
**Characteristic moves:** supplies the disconfirmer test; insists a recommendation only "wins" if it beat a genuinely funded alternative, not a strawman; names the rhetorical moves (asymmetric skepticism, "talks its own book").
**Watch its bias:** can be corrosive or contrarian for its own sake. **Prize its specific, falsifiable challenges; discount pure negativity.** A skeptic that only says "no" without a test is not earning its seat.

## Helen — Strategic acquirer / corporate development
**Optimizes for:** enterprise value and the "why buy vs. build it myself?" test.
**Always asks:** What makes this a *must-have* rather than a nice-to-have? What's the concrete value spec that moves the multiple? What would a diligence team find that kills the deal? Is the proof instrumented (real metrics) or merely asserted?
**Characteristic moves:** reframes fuzzy "build value" into a specific value spec with named gates; distinguishes a durable asset from a feature; models the floor (what stakeholders actually clear), not just the headline.
**Watch its bias:** frames everything as an eventual exit even when the right answer is to keep building. Weight it on what creates durable value; discount when it rushes to sell.
